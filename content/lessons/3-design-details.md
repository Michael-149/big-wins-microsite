## In the cases

**Search help beyond the voucher.** The [Moving to Opportunity](/case-studies/moving-to-opportunity) lottery, whose experimental vouchers came with counseling, showed that moving young children to lower-poverty areas could raise their later earnings, but not how to help families move. Creating Moves to Opportunity (CMTO), developed by two housing authorities with researchers, added housing navigators, a damage fund for landlords and help with deposits. In its first phase, in Seattle and King County from 2018 to 2019, 53% of families moved to high-opportunity areas (where low-income children have tended to do better as adults), against 15% of a lottery-picked control group with standard vouchers.

**A default that held savers in place.** From April 1998, a large company automatically enrolled new hires in its 401(k) retirement plan at 3% of pay in a money market fund (a low-risk investment). The first study of [automatic enrollment](/case-studies/behavioral-nudges) found 86% of them in the plan, against 37% of those hired just before, at equal time on the job, but 61% stayed at both defaults. From 2004 to 2007, regulators and Congress allowed and rewarded rising defaults and steered default money toward funds that grow more conservative with age.

**A cap in tons, measured at every unit.** In [emissions trading](/case-studies/emissions-trading), the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) let firms from 1976 offset new pollution with cuts elsewhere, but markets between firms stayed thin; a 1989 review blamed uncertain credits and the cost of finding partners and winning approval. Title IV of the 1990 Clean Air Act Amendments wrote the sulfur dioxide cap and each plant's allowances (permits to emit) into law, required no prior approval of trades, made every unit monitor emissions continuously and penalized excess emissions $2,000 a ton. Compliance was nearly 100%.

**Rules that worked, terms that did not.** For the Federal Communications Commission's (FCC's) [spectrum auctions](/case-studies/fcc-spectrum), Stanford economists Paul Milgrom and Robert Wilson, hired by bidders, proposed keeping bidding open on every license until the end, with an activity rule to stop bidders from holding back; economist Preston McAfee, then at the University of Texas at Austin, proposed a similar design. The FCC adopted it in 1994; the activity rule worked. But economists Jeremy Fox of the University of Michigan and Patrick Bajari of the University of Minnesota estimate that in a 1995 to 1996 auction for smaller firms, four large regional licenses instead of 493 small ones would have raised allocative efficiency (how well licenses go to the firms valuing them most) by 48%.

## The pattern

By operational details we mean choices about how a program, rule or market works, such as a default rate or license size, not the quality of its delivery or setting. In each listed case an earlier version lacked a key detail or got one wrong; for CMTO, that version was the standard voucher, which leaves the search to families. [Needle exchange](/case-studies/needle-exchange) is left out: researchers credit combined services there, but its history shows no detail corrected after a first version.

In the fifth case, a donor pledge to subsidize [pneumococcal vaccines](/case-studies/amc-vaccines) against a bacterium that kills young children, an expert group found the 2006 draft design unlikely to build enough factory capacity and recommended tying subsidies to long-term supply commitments. Evaluators found firms expanded capacity in response, some of it planned beforehand.

In automatic enrollment, CMTO and the auctions, the record ties results directly to a detail; in the other two, details worked alongside other causes. Sulfur costs came in far below forecasts mostly because of the flexible cap and cheaper coal rather than trading alone, and the vaccine commitment's final evaluators found it worked, though not as Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, had assumed.

Fixes were partial; some created problems. Sulfur trading moved allowances east to more populated areas; one study put program-wide 2002 damages $2.1 billion higher than with no trading (1995 dollars), though economists who helped shape the program find no significant local hot spots (pockets of high pollution), granting that damage-weighted rules could have done better. Paying vaccine makers by volume, evaluators say, helped keep price competition weak, along with a third supplier's late arrival.

## Why it is easy to miss

Decisive details were often recorded in places that rarely count as research: an Internal Revenue Service (IRS) letter, a Labor Department rule, a statute, an expert group's report to donors. By 2007, 3% was the most common automatic enrollment default, partly because IRS examples used it. Economist Esther Duflo, then at MIT, likens policy design to plumbing and argues such details often decide a policy's impact, yet policymakers "rarely have the time or inclination to focus on them" {{cite:duflo2017wp}}.

## What it means for funders and research leaders

- **Fund design with the people who will run it.** Pay for practitioners' time as co-designers, like CMTO's housing authorities, and ask what a design costs: CMTO's was $2,670 per family offered services.
- **Plan the sample to compare versions.** In CMTO's second phase, full services had five times the effect of information plus financial incentives. But in 2025 the federal housing department dropped a planned test of a cheaper version of its eight-region demonstration's services, concerned it could weaken the main one and strain sites already struggling to enroll families.
- **Test changes to what an agency already does.** Among 30 US cities that ran 73 trials with a national behavioral science team, the largest predictor of adoption, more than the strength of the evidence, was whether the trial used existing city communications {{cite:dellavigna2024}}.
- **Fund follow-up while rules are open.** The first automatic enrollment study circulated in 2000, before defaults were reshaped; the license-size estimate came long after. Require published design reasoning: Gavi's 2013 evaluators found the analyses behind the vaccine commitment's price not yet public.
- **Who can change a detail.** Title IV gave EPA no power to tighten the sulfur cap. Which detail could cancel your project's effect, and who could change it after launch?

## Where it may not hold

These are successes chosen after the fact, and eight cases cannot show how often a pattern holds. The set leans toward policy and market design, where research often enters through written rules, auctions, contracts or models, as in four of the five cases; such details are easy to trace and, in hindsight, to call decisive.

[Growth mindset](/case-studies/growth-mindset) programs, which teach that intelligence can grow, test this lesson most directly. Trials run or reported by developers found gains, mostly for struggling students, while independent ones found smaller gains or none, even in 101 English primary schools where teachers delivered the program as intended. Developers reply that large studies, some independent, justify confidence in the research. Two 2022 meta-analyses (pooled analyses of many studies) disagree on whether features such as targeting matter.

Details may not travel or move decisions. In that eight-region demonstration, moves to opportunity areas rose about 20 percentage points within a year, against about 38 in CMTO, a rough comparison, since the demonstration mainly serves existing voucher holders and defines opportunity areas differently. From 2017, counting only US damages cut federal values of the [social cost of carbon](/case-studies/dice-modeling) (the damage from one more ton of carbon dioxide) from $50 to $7 a ton for 2020 emissions (2018 dollars, discounting later harm at 3% a year), but whether such values change agencies' choices is unsettled.

## What others have found

This lesson echoes classic policy research. Political scientists Jeffrey Pressman of MIT and Aaron Wildavsky of the University of California, Berkeley, studied a federal economic development program in Oakland; their book's 1984 edition has a chapter titled "Implementation as Evolution" {{cite:pressman1984}}. Implementation scientists separate an intervention's "core components" from its "adaptable periphery," a line often found only "through trial and error over time" {{cite:damschroder2009}}.

On first versions, a team including Duflo reports designs that failed in India's regular schools but "still helped shape subsequent versions of the program" {{cite:banerjee2017}}. Design-based implementation research in education rests on "a commitment to iterative, collaborative design" {{cite:penuel2011}}.
